Password Security

Don't let one password unlock everything. Strong, unique passwords and a second step make a big difference.

🔑 Strong passwords

Longer beats clever

A password is only as good as it is hard to guess. Length matters most: aim for at least 12 characters, and longer is better.

Weak juan2005 password123

Names, birth years and common words are the first things attackers try. Short passwords can be guessed quickly.

Strong purple-tricycle-mango-rain MyDogEatsTwoBananasAtNoon!

A few random words, or a long sentence only you would think of, is long, hard to guess and still easy to remember. Make up your own: don't copy these examples.

Quick rules

  • Use a long password or a passphrase of several random words
  • Avoid names, birthdays, pet names, your school or anything on your social media
  • Use a different password for every account
  • Change a password if you think it was leaked or shared

🔄 Don't reuse passwords

One leak can open many doors

When a website is hacked, the stolen email and password lists are often tried on other sites. If you reused the same password, the attacker gets into those accounts too, and your email is the most dangerous one to lose.

A different password for each account keeps a single leak from spreading.

How one reused password spreads

  1. A small website you used gets hacked and your password leaks.
  2. The attacker tries the same email and password on your email, social media and other accounts.
  3. Every account that shares that password is now at risk.

🗝️ Password managers

Remember one, store the rest

Nobody can remember dozens of long, unique passwords. A password manager is an app that creates them, stores them securely and fills them in for you. You only need to remember one strong master password.

Many phones and browsers have one built in, and there are well-known standalone apps too. Choose one from a source you trust.

Using one safely

  • Make the master password long and unique, and never reuse it
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for the manager itself
  • Don't keep passwords in a notes app or a message to yourself
  • Start with your email and your most important accounts

🔐 Two-factor authentication

Something you know + something you have

Two-factor authentication (2FA) asks for a second proof that it's really you, even if someone has your password.

🔑 Password+📱 Verification code=Extra protection

Authenticator app

Creates a new short-lived code on your phone. A good, widely available choice.

Text message code

Sent to your phone number. Not as strong as an app, but much better than no second step.

Security key

A small physical key you plug in or tap. One of the strongest options, for accounts that matter most.

Never share your verification codes. Anyone who asks for one, even someone claiming to be support, is trying to get into your account. Save your backup codes somewhere safe in case you lose your phone.

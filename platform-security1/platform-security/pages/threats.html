Common Online Threats

Every threat below follows the same six steps: what it is, how it happens, the warning signs, a real-life example, how to prevent it, and what to do if it happens.

Phishing

Fake messages and websites that trick you into giving something away.

What is it?

Someone pretends to be a person or company you trust to get your passwords, codes or personal details, or to make you click something harmful.

How does it happen?

Through fake emails, text messages, chat messages, websites or login pages. They copy real logos and names, then pressure you to act fast.

Warning signs

  • Urgent or threatening language
  • Suspicious links or unexpected attachments
  • Requests for passwords or codes
  • A strange or slightly misspelled sender address

Real-life example

A text says "Your account will close in 1 hour. Verify now" and links to a look-alike login page. Anyone who types their password there hands it to the attacker.

How to prevent it

  • Check the sender's address, not just the name
  • Don't click: open the official app or website yourself
  • Verify unexpected requests another way
  • Turn on two-factor authentication

What to do if it happens

  1. Stop. Don't enter anything else or open attachments.
  2. If you typed a password, change it right away, and anywhere you reused it.
  3. Turn on two-factor authentication and check for activity you don't recognize.
  4. Report the message to the platform, your school or your IT team.
👤

Social Engineering

Manipulating people, not computers, to get access or information.

What is it?

Attackers use trust, fear, curiosity or helpfulness to talk people into sharing information or giving access. Phishing is one type of social engineering.

How does it happen?

Someone pretends to be tech support, a teacher, a boss or a friend, by phone or chat. They build trust, create pressure, and ask for something that seems small.

Warning signs

  • Pressure to act right now
  • Requests for passwords or verification codes
  • Someone claims authority but you can't verify it
  • An emotional story or an offer that is too good to be true

Real-life example

A message says "I'm from the school IT office. I need the code we just sent to your phone to fix your account." That code is the key to your login.

How to prevent it

  • Never share passwords or one-time codes with anyone
  • Check who they are through an official channel you find yourself
  • Slow down: real support won't rush you
  • Keep personal details off public profiles

What to do if it happens

  1. Stop talking to the person and don't send anything more.
  2. Change the passwords for any account you shared.
  3. Tell the real organization, teacher or IT team what happened.
  4. Block and report the account, and keep watching your accounts for changes.
🦠

Malware

Harmful software made to damage, spy on or take control of a device.

What is it?

"Malware" is short for malicious software. Viruses, ransomware and spyware are all types of malware.

How does it happen?

Usually when you download from an untrusted site, install pirated software, open an unexpected attachment, click a harmful ad or link, or plug in an infected USB drive.

Warning signs

  • Your device suddenly slows down or the battery drains fast
  • Apps you don't remember installing
  • Constant pop-ups or changed browser settings
  • Security tools that turned themselves off

Real-life example

A free "game crack" from an unknown website installs a hidden program that records what you type, including your passwords.

How to prevent it

  • Download only from official app stores and websites
  • Keep your system and apps updated
  • Use trusted security software
  • Skip pirated software and unexpected attachments

What to do if it happens

  1. Disconnect the device from the internet.
  2. Run a full scan with trusted security software.
  3. Remove apps you don't recognize.
  4. From a clean device, change your important passwords.
🚨

Viruses

Malicious programs that attach to files and spread when you open or share them.

What is it?

A virus is a type of malware that hides inside a file or program. When you run or share that file, it copies itself to other files and devices.

How does it happen?

Through infected downloads, email attachments and shared USB drives. The virus only spreads when someone opens or passes on the infected file.

Warning signs

  • Files that go missing, change or won't open
  • Programs that crash for no reason
  • Strange copies of files appearing
  • Alerts from your security software

Real-life example

A classmate lends you a flash drive. Opening one infected file copies the virus to your computer, and then to every other drive you plug in.

How to prevent it

  • Scan USB drives and downloads before opening them
  • Keep security software and your system updated
  • Don't open attachments you weren't expecting
  • Keep backups of important files

What to do if it happens

  1. Run a full scan and follow the removal steps your security software gives.
  2. Stop sharing files and drives from that device.
  3. Restore clean copies of your files from a backup.
  4. Tell anyone you may have passed infected files to.
🔒

Ransomware

Malware that locks your files and demands payment to unlock them.

What is it?

Ransomware scrambles (encrypts) your files so you can't open them, then shows a message demanding money to get them back.

How does it happen?

Most often through a phishing attachment, a harmful download or software that hasn't been updated. Once it runs, it can lock files within minutes.

Warning signs

  • Files suddenly won't open or have strange new names
  • A message on screen demanding payment
  • A deadline or threat to delete your files
  • A request to pay in cryptocurrency or gift cards

Real-life example

A student opens an attachment from a fake "invoice" email. The next morning every document shows a note demanding payment.

How to prevent it

  • Back up important files, with one copy kept offline or in the cloud
  • Keep your system and apps updated
  • Don't open unexpected attachments
  • Download only from sources you trust

What to do if it happens

  1. Disconnect the device from Wi-Fi and the internet right away.
  2. Tell your school, IT team or a trusted adult.
  3. Think twice before paying: it doesn't guarantee you get your files back.
  4. Clean the device, then restore your files from a backup.
💰

Online Scams

Fraud schemes that try to get your money or personal information.

What is it?

A scam is any online scheme built to trick you into sending money or sharing personal details. Common types are fake shops, fake prizes, fake jobs and fake investments.

How does it happen?

Through social media ads, direct messages, marketplace listings and copycat websites. Scammers create excitement or urgency, then ask for payment up front.

Warning signs

  • Prices or prizes that seem too good to be true
  • Pressure to pay quickly
  • Payment by gift card, crypto or a transfer to a personal account
  • New profiles with no history, or a push to move the chat somewhere private

Real-life example

A social media page sells a popular phone at half price. After you send the payment, the seller blocks you and the phone never arrives.

How to prevent it

  • Check the seller or shop, including reviews outside their own page
  • Use the platform's protected payment option when there is one
  • Never pay a fee to receive a prize or a job
  • If a deal feels too good to be true, take a day before deciding

What to do if it happens

  1. Stop sending money and stop replying.
  2. Contact your bank or e-wallet provider right away to report the payment.
  3. Keep screenshots and receipts, then report the account to the platform.
  4. Report it to the authorities. In the Philippines, that includes the PNP Anti-Cybercrime Group and the NBI Cybercrime Division.

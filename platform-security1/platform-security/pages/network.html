Every time you go online, your data travels over a network. Make sure that network is one you can trust.

🏠 Home Wi-Fi

Lock down your router

Your router is the front door to your home network. If someone gets into it, they may see what connects to it or slow down your connection, so it's worth a few minutes to set it up properly.

Router checklist

  • Use a strong Wi-Fi password: long, and different from your other passwords
  • Change the default router login (the admin name and password printed on the device)
  • Keep the router's firmware updated
  • Use WPA3 or WPA2 security, never "open" or WEP
  • Give visitors a guest network if your router offers one
  • Don't put your name or address in the network name

☕ Public Wi-Fi

Convenient, but be careful

Public Wi-Fi (cafés, malls, airports, schools) is shared with strangers, and you can't tell who set it up or who else is on it.

What can go wrong

  • Fake hotspots with a look-alike name, set up to catch what you do
  • Other people on the same network may be able to see unprotected traffic
  • Fake login pages shown before you get online

Don't panic

Most websites use HTTPS, which protects what you send to them. So public Wi-Fi isn't "always dangerous". The point is to be careful when you sign in to important accounts or enter private information.

🛡️ Network protection

Extra layers on any network

Firewall

Keep your computer's built-in firewall on. It blocks unwanted connections from outside.

Sharing off

Turn off file and device sharing when you're on a public network, and forget networks you no longer use.

VPN (optional)

A trusted VPN can add privacy on public networks, but it doesn't stop phishing or malware, so keep the other habits too.
